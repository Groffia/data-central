01 · What

Show the answer so people can act

Visualization makes complex data understandable so stakeholders can act. Data scientists build the charts, and business analysts present them.

Tools: Dashboard and BI tools, such as Power BI and Databricks AI/BI.

02 · How

Match the chart to the question

Each question has a chart that suits it: a number for how much, a line for change, bars for which is worst, and a limit line for when to act. Sample: Stage 6's answers go to an operations director.

Tools: Dashboard and BI tools, such as Power BI and Databricks AI/BI.

03 · Why

A chart is for a decision

People grasp a picture faster than a table. A chart that no decision depends on is noise. Analysts also chart while exploring, before this formal stage.

Tools: Dashboard and BI tools, such as Power BI and Databricks AI/BI.

04 · When

When an answer needs a decision

A chart is built once Stage 6 has an answer that people must act on, and it is refreshed as new data arrives.

Tools: Dashboard and BI tools, such as Power BI and Databricks AI/BI.

01 · What

One view for the reader

A dashboard brings the key numbers and charts together in one place, built for the person who decides. It refreshes as new data arrives.

Tools: Dashboard and BI tools, such as Power BI and Databricks AI/BI.

02 · How

Tiles first, then the detail

Tiles show the key numbers at a glance. Below them, a trend line shows whether it is getting worse and bars show which clinic. Sample: Clinic B is the one over the limit.

Tools: Dashboard and BI tools, such as Power BI and Databricks AI/BI.

03 · Why

So one look is enough

A reader should not have to dig. Every tile and chart answers one question for one reader, and anything that does not is left out.

Tools: Dashboard and BI tools, such as Power BI and Databricks AI/BI.

04 · When

When the reader opens it, or data changes

A dashboard is read on demand and refreshed as new data arrives. A real-time view updates continuously.

Tools: Dashboard and BI tools, such as Power BI and Databricks AI/BI.

05 · Next

Trace a number

A reader who sees 34 minutes will ask where it came from. Next: how any number on a dashboard can be traced back to its source.

Tools: Lineage and audit trail tools.

01 · What

Follow a number back

Traceability lets a reader follow any number on a dashboard back through the stages to the row it began as. Data stewards keep that record.

Tools: Lineage and audit trail tools.

02 · How

Click, then walk back

Click a tile and see its path: the definition that calculates it, the governed Silver rows, and the source the rows were collected from. Sample: 34 minutes traces to survey rows from Stages 1 and 2.

Tools: Lineage and audit trail tools.

03 · Why

Trust needs a trail

An answer nobody can trace back cannot be trusted. A trail also lets anyone check how a number was calculated before they act on it.

Tools: Lineage and audit trail tools.

04 · When

When a reader doubts a number

The trail is there whenever someone questions a figure, and every look is written to the audit log.

Tools: Lineage and audit trail tools.

05 · Next

Gate vs no gate

A trail shows where a number came from. Next: what a quality check does to the number a dashboard shows.

Tools: Data quality and dashboard tools.

01 · What

A gate protects the picture

A quality gate checks rows before they reach a chart. Rows that fail are held back, so the dashboard shows a real number, not a bad one.

Tools: Data quality tools.

02 · How

Same data, two views

Both dashboards read the same November data. Without the gate, six bad rows are counted. With it, they wait in a repair queue. Sample: 58 minutes against 34.

Tools: Data quality tools.

03 · Why

Garbage in, garbage out

A chart is only as good as the rows behind it. A bad number sends people to fix a problem that is not there.

Tools: Data quality tools.

04 · When

Every time data refreshes

The gate runs each time new rows arrive, before anything reaches a dashboard. A person works through the repair queue.

Tools: Data quality tools.

01 · What

Keep the picture current

A dashboard goes stale unless it refreshes. Refreshing on a schedule or live, and alerting when a number crosses a limit, keep readers acting on today's facts.

Tools: Orchestration and automation tools, such as Airflow and Power Automate.

02 · How

Refresh, compare, alert

Each refresh adds new points. A rule compares them with the limit, and a crossing sends an alert. Sample: Clinic B passes 32 minutes in week 5 and its lead is told.

Tools: Orchestration and automation tools, such as Airflow and Power Automate.

03 · Why

So nobody acts on yesterday

A stale view sends people after old problems. An alert reaches the right person without waiting for someone to open the dashboard.

Tools: Orchestration and automation tools, such as Airflow and Power Automate.

04 · When

On a schedule, or when a limit is crossed

Views refresh after each data check, or continuously for a real-time view. An alert fires the moment a rule's limit is crossed.

Tools: Orchestration and automation tools, such as Airflow and Power Automate.

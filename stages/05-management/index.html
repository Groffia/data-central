Stage 5 of 8 · Data management · Part 1 of 2

Who may see data, and what it means.

Management keeps stored data accurate, private and trusted. At Tamsworth Health, the clean Silver row from Stage 4 gets a description, rules about who may read it, and a record of every read.

Sample data for design review.

01 · What

Set the rules for the row

Governance decides who may see data and what it means. Data stewards, security and compliance own it. Five fields in the Silver row identify a patient, so they are marked sensitive.

Tools: Governance and catalog tools.

02 · How

Describe, restrict, record

A catalog says what each field means. Access control gives each role only what it needs and hides the rest. Every read is written to an audit log.

Tools: A catalog, access control and an audit log.

03 · Why

So the data can be trusted

Rules make data findable, understandable and trustworthy, so it is usable at speed. The log and the trace show where the row came from and who has read it.

Tools: Lineage and audit trail tools.

04 · When

At every request

Each time someone asks to read the row, the rules are checked, the answer is shaped to their role, and the request is logged. Quality is checked all the time, not once.

Tools: Access control and continuous quality checks.

05 · Next

Part 2: how long it lives

Next come the rules about time. The row is archived after 7 years, and deleted only when there is a reason, such as a request to be erased.

Tools: Retention and deletion policies.

01 · Where

Two places AI helps

Inside governance, where AI labels and describes data. Around it, where an agent watches every read.

02 · What

It labels and describes fields

AI reads each field, suggests whether it is sensitive, and writes a plain description for the catalog. This is metadata enrichment.

Tools: Classification and metadata enrichment.

03 · Why it matters

A steward confirms

A suggestion is not a change. A person confirms each label before it counts.

04 · Why it scales

An agent watches every read

It checks reads against the sharing policy all day and flags any that break it, such as a sensitive field shown to the wrong role.

05 · Next

People approve the fix

The agent proposes a fix and waits. A person approves it. Sources keep humans in control of key decisions.

01 · Where

The vector store needs rules too

The store from Stage 4 holds numbers for survey answers. It is governed like any other data.

02 · Describe

Say what it holds

The catalog records what the store holds, where it came from and who owns it, so people can find and trust it.

Tools: Catalog tools.

03 · Restrict

Retrieval follows access

When someone asks a question, the search returns only the chunks that person may read. A blocked chunk is never shown, even if it is the closest match.

Tools: Access control on the vector database.

04 · Record

Every search is logged

The log notes who asked and which chunks came back, so an answer can be traced to its source.

05 · Next

Part 2: how long it lives

Next: how long chunks are kept, and removing a person's chunks when they ask to be erased.

01 · What

Decide how long data lives

Retention sets how long data is kept and where. Archive keeps it for rare use; deletion removes it for good. Compliance and data stewards own the rules.

Tools: Retention policies.

02 · How

Keep, archive, delete only on cause

The row stays in the store for 7 years, then moves to the archive. Deletion happens only when something triggers it.

Tools: Retention policies.

03 · Why

Keeping is cheap; deleting is final

Storage costs little, so age alone is no reason to delete. Archive keeps data for audits and the law. Deleting protects people and cuts risk, so it needs a reason.

Tools: An archive store.

04 · When

On a trigger

A person asks to be erased, or a security or data-integrity concern is raised. Every copy of the data is found, removed and checked, and the deletion is logged.

Tools: A deletion workflow and the audit log.

Stage 6: Analysis

Data that is described, protected and kept on rules flows on to analysis, where it is turned into answers.

Tools: Machine learning and language models.

01 · Where

Two places AI helps

Inside retention, where AI finds what is due and where every copy lives. Around it, where an agent watches the rules.

02 · What

It finds what is due

AI scans for rows that have reached 7 years and proposes archiving them.

Tools: Monitoring and classification.

03 · Why it matters

It finds every copy

For an erasure it searches every place the data lives, including backups and the archive. People miss copies; the search does not tire.

04 · Why it scales

An agent watches retention

It checks all day that nothing is kept past its rule or deleted without a trigger, and flags anything that is.

05 · Next

People approve every deletion

Deleting is final, so the agent proposes and a person approves each one. Sources keep humans in control of key decisions.

01 · Where

Chunks follow the same rules

The vector store holds pieces of survey answers. They must age, archive and erase like the rows they came from.

02 · Archive

Chunks are archived with their row

When a row reaches 7 years, its chunks go to the archive with it. They are kept, not deleted.

Tools: A vector database.

03 · Erase

Erase the chunks, not just the row

When a person asks to be erased, their chunks must go too. A model could otherwise still answer from them.

Tools: A vector database and the deletion workflow.

04 · Check

Ask again and prove it

After the erasure the same question finds nothing about that person, and the log records what was removed.
